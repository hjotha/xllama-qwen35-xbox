# F7 verdict - weight-bandwidth bound (2026-09-28)

Measured attribution on app 1.0.0.42, d3d12 backend, dedicated bench:

| drafts | positions | draft compute mean |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 64,590 us |
| 4 | 5 | 64,516 us |

The draft compute is independent of the number of positions: the cycle streams
the draft weights once regardless of batch, so the draft is fully
weight-bandwidth bound. Operating point: ~917 MB of K-quant weights per cycle
at ~14.2 GB/s effective.

End-to-end model validated against production: TPS = tokens_per_cycle /
(xbox_cycle + target_side). With tokens_per_cycle 2.81, target side ~58 ms and
xbox cycle 74.2 ms this predicts 21.3 tok/s, matching the measured 21.26.

Baseline comparison (same workload, 512 tokens, seed 1234):
- Swift alone (4070, historical): 34.99 tok/s -> budget of ~22 ms for the
  entire Xbox side to break even.
- DFlash RPC CPU Xbox: 12.92 tok/s.
- DFlash RPC d3d12 Xbox: 21.26 tok/s (1.65x over CPU, still below Swift alone).

Viability conditions computed from the measured model:
- At the current acceptance (2.81 tokens/cycle) beating Swift alone requires
  the draft in <= ~13 ms -> ~70 GB/s effective weight bandwidth (5x the
  current 14.2 GB/s; beyond the project's measured gpugemv range of 25-40
  GB/s).
- At 4 tokens/cycle the budget becomes ~56 ms for the Xbox side and ~46 ms
  for the draft -> ~20 GB/s effective, which is within the gpugemv-measured
  range. The path to viability is therefore: gpugemv-class kernels (grouped
  u32 loads instead of per-lane byte loads in q3k_q8k / q4k_q8k, wave32
  variants) combined with acceptance at or above ~4 tokens/cycle (draft/target
  tuning, n_max already at its measured optimum of 4).

Conclusion: the GPU executor is a 1.65x win over the Xbox CPU path but not yet
a win over Swift alone on this 4070 setup. The remaining work is the kernel
bandwidth project above; no further Xbox-side change was validated in this
session beyond the integer-dot kernels, backend selection and diagnostics.
